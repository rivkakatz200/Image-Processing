
## Part 1

The problem is that cv2.imread normally loads a color image as np.uint8.

This means that every channel contains values between 0 and 255.

When we add two or three uint8 values, the result can be larger than 255. However, uint8 cannot store values larger than 255.

For example, if two values are 200 and 100, their real sum is 300, but a uint8 value cannot represent 300. The value can overflow and wrap around.

Therefore, the addition of the three channels can produce an incorrect result before the division by 3.

To solve the problem, we need to convert the values to a floating-point type before performing the addition.


## Part 4

It is important not to subtract two np.uint8 arrays directly.

The reason is that uint8 can only represent values from 0 to 255. If we subtract a larger value from a smaller value, the mathematical result is negative, but uint8 cannot represent negative numbers.

For example:
10 - 20 = -10

But uint8 cannot store -10, so the result can wrap around and become an incorrect large positive value.

Therefore, before subtracting, we convert the arrays to a floating-point type.

There is usually a visible difference between the two images, especially in areas with strong colors.

The reason is that the simple average gives equal importance to all three channels, while the weighted formula gives different importance to each channel.

The green channel has the largest weight, followed by red, and then blue.

Therefore, the brightness of some areas can be different between the two grayscale images.
